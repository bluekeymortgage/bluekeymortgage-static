Staying in your home

Stay in the Home You Love: How a Reverse Mortgage Can Work for You

By Ragini Domenichini, Mortgage Agent Level 2 (FSRA licence M22004226), BRX Mortgage · November 4, 2026 · 5-minute read

You've made your house a home. A reverse mortgage may let homeowners 55+ in Toronto, Mississauga and Durham turn part of its value into tax-free cash, with no required monthly mortgage payments and without selling.

What is a reverse mortgage?

A reverse mortgage is a loan for homeowners aged 55 and older that lets you borrow against your home's equity while you keep living in it. According to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC), you can usually access up to 55% of your home's value. The amount depends on your age, your home and its location, and the lender.

You keep ownership of your home. The loan is repaid when you sell, move out or the last borrower passes away.

Why GTA homeowners are looking at it

Stay in your home and your neighbourhood

No need to downsize, move away from family or leave the community you love.

Tax-free cash

FCAC notes that reverse mortgage funds are tax-free and don't affect Old Age Security (OAS) or the Guaranteed Income Supplement (GIS).

No required monthly mortgage payments

Free up your monthly cash flow for groceries, travel, home care or simply some breathing room.

Flexible ways to receive the money

Take a lump sum, regular scheduled advances or a mix of both, depending on the lender.

Help the people you love

Many families use part of their equity to help a child with a down payment, pay for a grandchild's education or celebrate a wedding, while they can see the joy it brings. See the living inheritance.

Pay off debts or fix up the home

Clear an existing mortgage, line of credit or credit cards, or make your home safer and more comfortable with accessibility updates.

Real-life ways families use it

  • A Mississauga couple wants to travel and visit grandchildren out west without watching every dollar.
  • A widow in Scarborough wants to stay in the family home and hire help with the yard and snow.
  • A Durham homeowner wants to retire an existing mortgage payment so a pension can stretch further.
  • Adult children want Mom and Dad to stay safe and independent at home, and want to understand the options with them.

Illustrative examples, not actual clients.

The honest part, in brief

A reverse mortgage is a loan, so it's worth going in with clear eyes. Interest is added to the balance over time, there are set-up, appraisal and legal costs, and the equity left later will usually be lower. You keep title to your home and simply carry on with what you already do as a homeowner: pay your property taxes and home insurance and keep up the home. Every borrower gets independent legal advice before signing, and families are always welcome in the conversation.

Is it right for you?

A reverse mortgage can be a great fit if you:

  • are 55 or older (everyone on title) and own your home in the GTA
  • plan to stay in your home for the long term
  • want more monthly breathing room or a lump sum for something meaningful
  • prefer not to take on a loan with required monthly payments

Sometimes a home equity line of credit, refinancing or downsizing fits better, and I'll walk you through those too. My job is to help you find what fits, not to push a product.

Questions people ask about this

Who can get a reverse mortgage in Ontario?

Usually homeowners aged 55+ (everyone on title) whose home is their primary residence. Each lender has its own property and location requirements, and approval is up to the lender.

How much can I access?

Usually up to 55% of your home's appraised value, depending on your age, your home and the lender.

Do I have to make monthly payments?

No monthly mortgage payments are required. You can choose to make payments if you like, depending on your lender's terms.

Is the money taxable? Will it affect my OAS or GIS?

According to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, reverse mortgage funds are tax-free and don't affect OAS or GIS.

Can I lose my home?

Not because of the loan itself, as long as you keep up your property taxes, home insurance and upkeep.

Do I still own my home?

Yes. You stay on title, and any growth in your home's value benefits you.

What happens for my family later?

When the home is sold, the loan is repaid and the remaining equity goes to you or your estate. Your children are welcome to join our conversation so everyone is on the same page.

Is a reverse mortgage a government program?

No. Reverse mortgages are offered by private lenders. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada publishes neutral information about them.
