Understand the agreement before you sign

Do You Need a Lawyer for a Reverse Mortgage in Ontario?

By Ragini Domenichini, Mortgage Agent Level 2 · October 26, 2026 · 8 minute read

The legal appointment is not just another form to sign. Here is why independent legal advice matters, how it differs from closing the mortgage, and what to ask before making a decision about your home.

The short answer: plan for independent legal advice

If you are considering a reverse mortgage in Ontario, the lawyer is not an optional last minute signature service. Independent legal advice, often called ILA, is there to help you understand what you are agreeing to before your home is used as security.

For a reverse mortgage arranged through a mortgage brokerage, section 29 of Ontario Regulation 188/08 requires the brokerage to receive a lawyer signed statement confirming that the borrower received independent legal advice about the proposed mortgage. FSRA, Ontario's mortgage brokering regulator, specifically reminds brokerages of this duty.

Lenders also have requirements. EQ Bank, for example, says its reverse mortgage clients must obtain ILA, including applicable spouses who are not on title. The Ontario brokerage rule and a lender's instructions are related, but they are not the same thing. Confirm the process for the particular mortgage you are considering.

More than signing: this is your chance to ask what the agreement means for you, your home, and your future choices. You should not feel rushed simply because the application or appraisal is already complete.

What does independent legal advice actually cover?

An independent lawyer explains the legal effect of the proposed agreement and your responsibilities. The Canadian Bar Association describes independence as having no conflict of interest with the people signing the transaction. It warns that ILA is not a rubber stamp.

Bring your real concerns, not just the documents. Perhaps you expect to move in a few years, want to help an adult child, or worry about what happens if one spouse needs care. Ask the lawyer to explain how the written terms apply to those possibilities.

  • When can the lender require repayment, including after a sale, permanent move, death, or default?
  • What happens to the balance as interest is added, and which charges can apply?
  • What would paying off the mortgage early involve?
  • Which property tax, insurance, maintenance, and occupancy obligations continue?
  • How could the mortgage affect a spouse, ownership arrangements, or the estate?
  • What do any guarantees or protections actually require, and what are their limits?

For the ongoing budget questions, read who pays property taxes and insurance. For a possible move, start with paying off a reverse mortgage early, then have the lawyer explain your own agreement.

The mortgage agent, closing lawyer, and ILA lawyer have different jobs

As your mortgage agent, I can help compare available mortgage options, explain the application process, and identify costs and questions to confirm. That does not make me your lawyer, and a conversation with me does not replace independent legal advice.

The closing work is about completing the transaction. Depending on the arrangement, it may involve registering the mortgage, paying an existing secured loan, and accounting for the funds. ILA is about independently advising you on the proposed agreement. Ask who is responsible for each role.

EQ Bank's current ILA explainer describes a closing lawyer who represents both the borrower and the bank. In that arrangement, it requires a separate lawyer with no conflicting interest to provide ILA. Do not assume that one appointment or one legal invoice covers both jobs. Other arrangements must be checked against the lender's instructions and the lawyers' professional obligations.

If you already have a lawyer, ask whether they handle reverse mortgage transactions, which role they could take, and whether another lawyer is needed. A referral can help you find someone, but you should understand who the lawyer represents and be comfortable asking questions.

Ask for a written legal fee quote

There is no universal legal fee in this guide. The work, ownership situation, lender process, and lawyer's quote can differ. EQ Bank says clients are responsible for their ILA fees. FCAC also lists legal fees and closing costs among possible reverse mortgage expenses.

Before booking, ask:

  • Is this quote for independent legal advice, closing the mortgage, or both?
  • Are taxes and disbursements included? Disbursements are expenses paid on your behalf.
  • Could a separate spouse appointment, additional documents, or extra advice change the price?
  • Who pays each charge, and when is it due?
  • What would I owe if I decide not to proceed or the mortgage does not fund?

Compare the legal quote with the lender's cost disclosure so you know which amounts are included and do not count the same charge twice. Ask for the expected cash remaining after any old mortgage payout, fees, and other required deductions. An approved borrowing amount is not necessarily the amount available to spend.

Prepare for a useful, unhurried meeting

Ask the lawyer what they need before the appointment. Confirm that they have the current proposed mortgage documents, not an outdated illustration or an earlier offer with different terms.

Keep your questions on one page. Bring the identification and documents the lawyer requests, and explain any relevant ownership, spouse, existing mortgage, or signing arrangements. If someone proposes using a power of attorney, flag that early and let the lender and lawyer confirm what is permitted and required.

Expect an opportunity to speak privately. EQ Bank describes an ILA meeting with no other person present. The Canadian Bar Association also recommends meeting the client alone and using an interpreter who is not a family member when one is needed. Ask in advance about language, hearing, mobility, or other communication needs.

An adult child can help you gather questions beforehand. That is different from answering for you or pressuring you to sign. If you are uncomfortable with a gift, a family request, or the mortgage itself, tell the lawyer privately.

Before leaving, try explaining the main obligations back in your own words. Ask about anything you cannot explain. Confirm what written advice or documents you will receive, who sends the required confirmation, and whether any unanswered issue means the transaction should wait.

A legal certificate is not a promise that the mortgage suits you

Independent legal advice is an important protection. It does not turn a loan into free money, guarantee approval, or mean that borrowing the maximum is wise.

FCAC notes that reverse mortgage interest is added to the balance, increasing what you owe over time. Rates are usually higher than conventional mortgages or HELOCs, and there may be less equity for a later move or an inheritance. You still need a workable plan for the home's ongoing expenses.

Compare realistic alternatives, including an affordable conventional mortgage or HELOC if you qualify, using savings carefully, reducing the amount borrowed, or downsizing. Legal advice and a broader financial review answer different questions. Neither replaces the need to understand your own budget and priorities.

FSRA's guidance also makes clear that the brokerage has responsibilities around suitability and disclosures. Those responsibilities do not disappear because a lawyer signed an ILA statement.

Questions people ask about this

Is independent legal advice required for a reverse mortgage in Ontario?

For a reverse mortgage arranged through a mortgage brokerage, section 29 of Ontario Regulation 188/08 requires the brokerage to receive a lawyer signed statement confirming the borrower received independent legal advice about the proposed mortgage. Lenders also set their own requirements. Confirm the process for your proposed loan.

What does a reverse mortgage lawyer explain?

Ask the lawyer to explain the agreement, your obligations, when repayment can be required, early repayment charges, and the effect on your home and estate. Independent legal advice also gives you a private opportunity to raise pressure, uncertainty, or questions before signing.

Can the closing lawyer also give me independent legal advice?

Do not assume so. The lawyer giving independent advice must have no conflicting interest. EQ Bank's current explainer describes a closing lawyer acting for both the borrower and bank, with a separate lawyer providing ILA. Confirm the arrangement and requirements for your own transaction before booking.

How much does independent legal advice cost?

There is no single fee quoted in this article. Obtain a written quote for your situation and ask whether it covers only ILA or also any closing work, taxes, and disbursements. Confirm who pays, when payment is due, and what further work could cost. EQ Bank says its clients pay their ILA fees.

Does a spouse who is not on title need legal advice?

The lender's requirements and your circumstances matter. EQ Bank requires ILA for applicable spouses who are not on title as well as borrowers. Tell the lender and lawyer about your spouse and ownership arrangements rather than assuming only the person registered as owner needs advice.

Does an ILA certificate mean the reverse mortgage is the right choice?

No. A certificate records that independent legal advice was given; it is not a guarantee of affordability, approval, future equity, or suitability. Compare the full borrowing cost, remaining household budget, alternatives, and plans for moving or leaving an estate before deciding.

A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against your home. It must be repaid when you sell your home, move out permanently, or pass away. Interest accrues over time and reduces the equity remaining in your home. Terms, conditions, and lender qualification apply; approval, rates, and amounts are never guaranteed. A reverse mortgage is not a government program or benefit.
