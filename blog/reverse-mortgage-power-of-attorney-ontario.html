A careful next step for families

Can a Power of Attorney Apply for a Reverse Mortgage in Ontario?

By Ragini Domenichini, Mortgage Agent Level 2 (FSRA licence M22004226), BRX Mortgage Inc. (FSRA #13463) · November 23, 2026 · 7 minute read

Quick answer: Sometimes. A power of attorney for property may be used for a reverse mortgage application, but the document must give the necessary authority and the lender must accept the arrangement. A personal care power of attorney alone is not enough. Start with a lawyer's review and a lender check, not an assumption that the funds will be available.

If you help a parent with appointments and bills, handling the next piece of paperwork can feel like a natural step. A mortgage needs a little more care. Here is how to prepare the conversation while keeping the homeowner's needs and wishes at the centre.

Helping with paperwork is not the same as having authority to sign

You can help someone write down questions or understand a process without making their decisions for them. But signing a mortgage on their behalf is different. In Ontario, being a spouse or adult child does not automatically give you authority to make another person's financial decisions.

A power of attorney is a legal document that names someone to act on the person's behalf. The person appointed is called an attorney, even if they are not a lawyer. For an Ontario homeowner considering a reverse mortgage, the first question is not simply “Do we have a POA?” It is “Does this document allow this person to act for this transaction, now?”

You are not expected to work that out alone. Tell the mortgage agent early that a power of attorney may be involved, then have the document reviewed by a lawyer before planning around an approval or a closing date.

Property and personal care powers of attorney do different jobs

Ontario separates authority for property and financial affairs from authority for personal care. A personal care document may cover decisions about health care or living arrangements. It is not a substitute for authority to borrow against a home.

Ask the lawyer to review the property document, including any limits on borrowing, dealing with real estate or when the attorney can act. If more than one person is named, ask whether they must act together. Do not assume one sibling can sign because the other is unavailable.

Ownership stays separate: Being named as attorney does not make you the owner of the house or the money. Your role is to act within the authority given, not to treat the homeowner's equity as your own.

A valid document and a lender's acceptance are separate checks

EQ Bank's reverse mortgage FAQ says a property power of attorney may be used when the attorney has authority to deal with real property. It also says it will not permit this arrangement simply because the homeowner is out of the country. That is an example of a specific lender rule, not a promise that every lender follows the same policy.

The Law Society of Ontario's real estate guidelines tell lawyers to review a power of attorney and its restrictions, and to confirm the lender will accept documents signed under it well before closing. Title insurance acceptance may also need to be checked. These reviews help protect the homeowner and the transaction.

A document that a bank accepts for paying bills is not, by itself, confirmation that a reverse mortgage lender will accept it. Ask for the proposed signing process to be reviewed before paying for an appraisal or committing to an expense that depends on the mortgage funds. Acceptance of the signing arrangement is still not a loan approval.

A continuing power of attorney does not mean someone has lost capacity

Some people appoint help while they remain capable of making their own decisions. A continuing power of attorney can allow authority to continue if capacity is later lost, while a noncontinuing one may not. The document may also set conditions for when authority begins. Have a lawyer explain what the actual document does.

The Government of Canada notes that a capable person can continue making their own financial decisions after appointing an attorney. Keep them involved, give them time to ask questions and do not treat a relative's preference as their consent.

If there is uncertainty about capacity, a missing document or a disagreement about who can act, pause and get legal advice. Do not assume a new signature will solve the problem. This article cannot determine anyone's capacity or tell a family whether a particular power of attorney is valid.

Borrowing still needs to serve the homeowner's needs

A property attorney has responsibilities to the person they represent, including managing their affairs appropriately and keeping records. Before borrowing, write down the purpose, the amount needed and the alternatives considered. Keep the lender's illustrations and the answers to your questions with the records of the decision.

For example, if the goal is paying for support at home, compare the likely care budget with the borrowing cost and the funds left after any existing mortgage and fees. If someone proposes a gift to a family member, repayment of an attorney's own expenses or any personal benefit to the attorney, disclose it to the lawyer. Do not assume the power of attorney permits it.

A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against the home, not a benefit or free money. Interest added to the balance can leave less equity for a later move or the estate. Appraisal, legal and other fees matter too. Compare alternatives such as using other available resources, a smaller borrowing amount or moving rather than borrowing.

Also discuss who will handle taxes, insurance and upkeep, and what happens if the homeowner later moves out. A reverse mortgage usually becomes due on a sale, a permanent move, the last borrower's death or default, subject to the agreement. If care needs may change soon, read the guide to moving into long term care and ask the lender how its terms apply.

A short checklist before the application moves ahead

  • Explain who is involved. Tell the mortgage agent who owns the home, who lives there and who proposes to act under a power of attorney.
  • Arrange document review. Ask which document and supporting information the lawyer needs, including any conditions or other named attorneys.
  • Check lender acceptance early. Confirm the process for this lender and transaction, including who must sign and attend legal appointments.
  • Clarify independent advice. Ask who needs separate legal advice, which lawyer does which work and what fees may be payable even if the loan does not proceed. Our independent legal advice guide explains the different roles.
  • Review the money and the next move. Ask for the cash left after required payouts and fees, an illustration of the growing balance, and repayment terms if circumstances change.
  • Share documents securely. Verify the lawyer or mortgage agent before sending identity, mortgage, legal or health information. Do not put private documents in a public comment or social message.

Taking an extra step at the beginning can prevent a difficult surprise later. The aim is not simply to get paperwork signed. It is to understand the homeowner's options and make a decision that respects their rights and circumstances.

Questions families ask

Can I use a power of attorney to apply for my parent's reverse mortgage?

It may be possible if you have valid authority over property and the lender accepts the arrangement. The document, any restrictions and when you may act need legal review. Being an adult child does not by itself give you authority to sign for a parent.

Is a power of attorney for personal care enough to sign mortgage documents?

No. Ontario distinguishes personal care decisions from property and financial decisions. Ask a lawyer to review the property authority needed for the proposed mortgage rather than relying on a personal care document.

Does having a power of attorney mean the homeowner has lost capacity?

No. Some property powers of attorney can be used while the homeowner remains capable. Others start only when specified conditions are met. The document and circumstances need review; a family member should not make assumptions about capacity or when authority begins.

Can an attorney apply just because the homeowner is outside Canada?

Do not assume so. EQ Bank's reverse mortgage FAQ says it does not permit a power of attorney application simply because the homeowner is out of the country. That is a lender specific policy. Confirm the proposed signing arrangement before making travel or closing plans.

Does a power of attorney transfer ownership of the home?

No. The Government of Canada explains that an attorney manages money and property on the owner's behalf and does not become the owner. A proposed mortgage is a separate decision that still needs the appropriate authority, lender review and legal advice.

A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against your home. It generally becomes due when you sell your home, move out permanently, the last borrower dies, or you default under the agreement. Interest accrues over time and reduces the equity remaining in your home. Terms, conditions, and lender qualification apply; approval, rates, and amounts are never guaranteed. A reverse mortgage is not a government program or benefit.
