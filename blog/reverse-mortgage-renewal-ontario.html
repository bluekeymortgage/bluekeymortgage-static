A clear plan for the next rate term

Reverse Mortgage Renewal in Ontario: What Happens When Your Rate Term Ends?

By Ragini Domenichini, Mortgage Agent Level 2 · November 2, 2026 · 8 minute read

The end of an interest rate term is not automatically the end of your reverse mortgage. Here is how to read renewal information, compare the terms that actually apply to you, and ask about costs before making a decision.

A rate term ending is not the same as the loan becoming due

A letter about your reverse mortgage renewal can sound worrying. If it mentions the end of a five year term, does that mean you suddenly have to find the money to repay the whole loan?

The important distinction is between the interest rate term and the events that make the mortgage repayable. A rate term sets the period for particular interest rate arrangements. The mortgage agreement sets out when repayment is required. They are not interchangeable.

HomeEquity Bank publishes rates that apply when existing reverse mortgage contracts reset. EQ Bank also separates first time and renewing customers in its reverse mortgage rate information. Those renewal arrangements are different from simply assuming the loan ends when a rate period ends.

FCAC identifies repayment events including selling the home, moving out, the last borrower's death, and default. Your own agreement and circumstances matter. A renewal letter, a request for information, and a demand for repayment are different documents, so read what you have received carefully.

Start with one question: “Is this a change to my interest rate term, a proposed change to my loan, or a notice that repayment is required?” If the answer is not clear, ask the lender and get independent legal advice before making assumptions.

Find the rate term date in your own documents

Start with the mortgage agreement, latest statement, and any renewal or rate reset correspondence. Note the product name, current balance, current rate, and the date the existing rate arrangements end.

Then ask which choices are available for your particular contract. Do not assume the terms offered to a neighbour, or displayed in a new customer advertisement, apply to you.

HomeEquity Bank's posted rate pages, for example, distinguish products and contract groups. EQ Bank explains that its renewing customer category also covers certain later advances in the second term or beyond. That is a reminder to identify the transaction as well as the lender.

If you have not received information, contact the lender directly. Confirm how notices are sent and whether its contact details for you are current. Ask what, if anything, you need to do, by which date, and what happens if you do not choose an option. There is no universal automatic renewal process promised in this guide.

Do not compare your renewal with the wrong advertised rate

A headline rate may be limited to a new mortgage, a particular product, or qualifying circumstances. A rate for a new borrower is not automatically a renewal offer for an existing borrower.

For a useful comparison, ask for the rate that applies to your contract, the length of the proposed term, whether the rate is fixed or adjustable, and any applicable fees. Confirm when the rate is set and whether the offer can change before it takes effect.

Also ask how interest is calculated and added to the loan. A fixed rate and an adjustable rate have different risks. A shorter term is not automatically cheaper overall, and a longer term is not automatically the best protection if your plans may change.

This article intentionally does not quote today's rates. A number copied into a blog can become outdated and may not apply to your situation. The plain English interest rate guide explains the basics; your written renewal offer supplies the figures to review.

Review the growing balance, not just the monthly payment

It is easy to focus on the fact that regular mortgage repayments may not be required. But a rate change can still affect the cost of keeping the mortgage.

FCAC explains that interest is added to a reverse mortgage balance, so the amount owed increases over time. A higher rate can make that balance grow faster, even if there is no new monthly mortgage payment coming out of your bank account.

Ask the lender for an updated illustration using the actual balance and proposed terms. Have it separate existing debt, new borrowing, interest, and fees. Ask what assumptions it uses about future advances, voluntary payments, and rate changes. An illustration is not a guarantee of future debt or remaining equity.

Then consider the effect on your next move and your estate. Your home's future value is uncertain, and appreciation is not guaranteed to offset the growing loan. Keeping access to the home today and preserving equity for later are both worth discussing.

The household budget still needs to cover property taxes, home insurance, upkeep, and other ownership costs. A rate renewal does not remove those responsibilities.

Renewing, refinancing, and taking another advance are different decisions

Renewing or resetting the rate term concerns the interest arrangements on an existing reverse mortgage. Ask exactly what stays the same and what changes.

Refinancing or changing lenders can be a separate transaction involving a payout of the old loan and a new lending decision. Ask about qualification, valuation, legal work, discharge charges, other fees, and the cash left after those costs. A lower quoted rate alone does not prove a switch saves money.

Requesting more money is another question again. FCAC warns that additional advances may involve fees or a rate change. Do not assume a renewal automatically releases more cash or guarantees future borrowing.

If you are considering an additional advance, ask how it affects the existing balance and rate, whether new approval is needed, and what conditions apply. Our guide to lump sums and monthly advances can help you prepare those questions.

Planning to sell or repay? Confirm the payout rules before choosing a term

Think about what might happen during the proposed rate term. You may expect to downsize, move closer to family, make a voluntary payment, or use other funds to repay the mortgage.

Do not assume the end of an interest rate term is automatically a penalty free payout date. Reverse mortgage prepayment arrangements can depend on the lender and contract, how long the loan has been in place, the reset date, and any required notice.

EQ Bank's FAQ, for example, describes particular repayment opportunities linked to time since the initial advance, a reset date, or advance written notice. That does not create the same entitlement under every reverse mortgage.

Before relying on a payout amount, request a written statement for the exact payment date. Ask what is included, when it expires, and which notice or other conditions must be met. Read paying off a reverse mortgage early in Ontario for the fuller checklist.

If selling or moving is likely, compare the total cost of the choices available, not just the lowest displayed rate. Staying with the existing lender, changing the loan, reducing borrowing, or selling may each have tradeoffs. None is automatically the right answer.

A practical checklist for the renewal conversation

  • What date do my current interest rate arrangements end?
  • Which product, contract, rate, and term options apply to me as an existing customer?
  • What changes, and what continues unchanged, under the proposed renewal?
  • Are there fees, documents to provide, signatures, or response deadlines?
  • What happens if I do not make a selection?
  • How would the proposed terms affect the balance, using clearly stated assumptions?
  • What would an extra advance, voluntary payment, sale, or lender change involve?
  • Which conditions and notice periods affect a payout on my intended date?

Keep the lender's answers and your renewal documents together. Use contact details from your existing records or the lender's verified website, rather than an unexpected message asking for personal information.

Take time to ask questions before accepting a change. If the paperwork changes legal obligations or raises concerns about ownership, a spouse, or repayment, speak with an independent lawyer. The independent legal advice guide explains the different professional roles.

Questions people ask about this

Do I have to repay a reverse mortgage when a five year term ends?

The end of a five year interest rate term does not, by itself, mean the entire reverse mortgage must be repaid. The rate term and the events that make the loan due are different parts of the agreement. Confirm your contract, renewal arrangements, and whether a repayment event such as a sale, permanent move, death of the last borrower, or default has occurred.

Will my renewal rate match a new customer's advertised rate?

Not necessarily. HomeEquity Bank publishes reset rates for existing contracts, and EQ Bank distinguishes first time and renewing customers. Rates can depend on the product, contract, transaction, and date. Ask for the written rate and terms that apply to your own loan rather than relying on a promotion.

Does a higher renewal rate mean a new monthly mortgage payment?

A higher rate can increase the interest added to the balance even when regular mortgage repayments are not required under your agreement. It does not remove your property tax, insurance, maintenance, or other obligations. Check the written terms and ask for an updated balance projection.

Is renewing the same as refinancing or borrowing more money?

No. Reviewing the rate term on an existing loan is different from changing the loan or lender, or requesting an additional advance. Those changes can involve separate approval, conditions, and costs. Ask the lender which transaction is being proposed and what it would mean for the balance and available cash.

Can I pay off the mortgage without a charge on the renewal date?

Do not assume so. Reverse mortgage prepayment rules can depend on the lender, contract, time since the initial advance, reset date, and notice given. Ask for a written payout statement for the exact payment date and confirm any conditions for a charge to be reduced or waived.

What should I do if I have not received renewal information?

Contact the lender using contact details from your existing mortgage records or its verified website. Confirm the rate term expiry date, your options, any response deadline, and what happens if you do not make a selection. Do not assume the old rate continues or that every lender handles an unanswered notice the same way.

A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against your home. It must be repaid when you sell your home, move out permanently, or pass away. Interest accrues over time and reduces the equity remaining in your home. Terms, conditions, and lender qualification apply; approval, rates, and amounts are never guaranteed. A reverse mortgage is not a government program or benefit.
