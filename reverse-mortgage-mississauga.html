Reverse mortgages for Mississauga homeowners

Whether it's a Port Credit bungalow, a Streetsville semi or a condo near Square One, many Mississauga homeowners have built up real equity over decades. If you'd love to stay put and free up some of that value, let's talk about whether a reverse mortgage fits.

Quick answer: A reverse mortgage lets Mississauga homeowners 55+ turn part of their home's value into tax-free cash with no regular mortgage payments, while staying on title. Each part of Mississauga has its own housing mix, and that shows up in how a home is appraised.

Port Credit & Lakeview

Lakeside character homes and newer condos, with strong long-term demand.

Streetsville & Meadowvale

Malton & Cooksville

Long-standing neighbourhoods with many multi-generational homes.

Mississauga specifics

Condos, multi-generational homes and lakeside properties

Condos in the City Centre are generally eligible with most reverse mortgage lenders, though the lender reviews the building and condo fees. Many Mississauga families live multi-generationally; family members can live with you, and I'll explain how that's handled on the application.

Lakeside homes in Port Credit and Lakeview can involve extra appraisal steps. The City of Mississauga also offers property tax relief options for eligible low-income seniors; it's worth checking with the City before deciding, and I'll happily compare every option with you, not just a reverse mortgage.

Mississauga questions

Questions Mississauga homeowners ask most

Can my adult children live with me in Mississauga if I have a reverse mortgage?

Generally yes. The home must stay your principal residence, and other family members can live there with you. Lenders may ask about other occupants, so we'll cover it upfront.

Is my Square One area condo eligible?

Often yes. Most reverse mortgage lenders accept condos, subject to a review of the building and condo fees. Bring your status certificate details and I'll check options across lenders.

How much could I access?

It depends on your age, your home's appraised value and current lender terms, so I won't guess without those details. In a free chat I'll show you real figures in writing for your specific home. You can also try the estimator first.

Do I have to make monthly payments?

Regular mortgage payments are generally not required. You keep paying property taxes, home insurance and upkeep, and the loan is repaid when you sell, move out permanently, or the last borrower passes away. Interest is added to the balance over time, so I'll show you how that works before you decide.

A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against your home. It must be repaid when you sell your home, move out permanently, or pass away. Interest accrues over time and reduces the equity remaining in your home. Terms, conditions, and lender qualification apply; approval, rates, and amounts are never guaranteed. A reverse mortgage is not a government program or benefit.

The information on this website is general education, not legal, tax, or investment advice. Please consult an independent legal or financial advisor about your personal situation. Independent legal advice is required before completing any reverse mortgage in Ontario.
