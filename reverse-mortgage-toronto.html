Local knowledge

Toronto's neighbourhoods, and what they mean for a reverse mortgage

Toronto is really the former cities of Toronto, North York, Scarborough, Etobicoke, York and East York stitched together, and it isn't one housing market but many. I've worked with homeowners across the city, and each area brings its own character to the appraisal and lending process.

Old Toronto and the core

From condos along the transit corridors to character homes in Riverdale, The Beach and High Park. Condo reserve funds and heritage designations both get extra attention during underwriting.

North York

Family sized homes and bungalows in Willowdale, Don Mills and beyond, many owned for decades, often with substantial built up equity.

Scarborough

Bungalows and detached homes from Agincourt outward, across a wide range of values, with strong, settled homeowner communities.

Etobicoke

Everything from The Kingsway to postwar bungalows in the inner suburbs and waterfront homes in Mimico, which can carry unique appraisal considerations.

East York and Leaside

Character homes in Leaside and well kept bungalows on smaller, established lots, long held by the same families.

York

Dense, leafy family neighbourhoods with strong, steady resale demand.

Toronto specifics

House rich and cash tight, condos, and heritage homes

Many Toronto homeowners 55+ have lived for decades in bungalows and detached homes across the inner suburbs, while downtown and the transit corridors hold a lot of condos. High property values can mean substantial equity, and also higher property taxes, which is how so many longtime owners end up house rich and cash tight. A reverse mortgage is one way to turn some of that built up value into tax free cash while you stay in the home. If you want the full picture of how it works, start with my Ontario reverse mortgage guide, or weigh it against a line of credit in reverse mortgage versus HELOC in Ontario.

Toronto also has a high share of condo ownership and heritage designated homes, both of which add a step to the process. Condo lenders review the building's reserve fund and any resale restrictions; heritage designation gets factored into the appraisal. Neither one rules out a reverse mortgage, but both deserve attention up front rather than a surprise partway through.

I work with lenders experienced in Toronto's market conditions, so these details get handled early, not discovered late. You can also try my reverse mortgage calculator for an illustrative range, which is an estimate only, not a quote.

A separate city program worth knowing

City of Toronto property tax relief for eligible seniors

Separate from anything to do with a reverse mortgage, the City of Toronto offers property tax relief for eligible seniors, including a Property Tax Increase Cancellation and a Property Tax Increase Deferral, along with related water and solid waste relief. These are city programs with their own eligibility rules. They are not administered by Blue Key, and a reverse mortgage is not a government program or benefit.

Based on the city's 2026 materials, checked on November 16, 2026, the general picture is:

  • Income. Combined household income must not exceed $62,000 for 2026.
  • Increase Cancellation. Also requires a residential assessment below $975,000, and generally that you are 65 or older, or age 60 to 64 and receiving the Guaranteed Income Supplement (or, if widowed, the Spouse's Allowance under the Old Age Security Act).
  • Increase Deferral. Income cap of $62,000, and generally that you are 65 or older, or 60 to 64 with GIS or the OAS allowance, or 50 or older and receiving a pension or pension annuity under the Income Tax Act.
  • Ownership and account standing. You must have owned and occupied the home as your principal residence for one year or more before October 31, 2026, with no prior year property tax arrears and your water and utility account paid in full. You must reapply each year.

Please note the timing. The deadline to apply for the 2026 tax year was November 2, 2026, so that date has already passed. These programs usually run each year with updated income limits and rules, so the best move now is to check the city's page for the next year's dates and details. Full details and the application are on the City of Toronto property tax and utility relief page.

I would not promise that a reverse mortgage and these city programs can be combined. They are separate, the city sets the eligibility, and the sensible path is to confirm the current rules with the City of Toronto and mention both to an independent legal advisor before you decide anything.

Toronto questions

Questions Toronto homeowners ask most

Does a reverse mortgage affect Toronto's property tax deferral program?

The two are separate. A reverse mortgage is a private loan, while the city's tax relief programs are administered by the City of Toronto with their own eligibility rules. I would not promise they can be combined. Check current details with the city and mention both to your independent legal advisor before deciding.

Can Toronto condo owners get a reverse mortgage?

Often yes, though condos involve extra review, such as the building's reserve fund and any rules that affect resale. Lenders assess each condo individually, so I look into your specific building before we go further.

What about a heritage designated home in Toronto?

A heritage designation does not prevent a reverse mortgage, but it can affect the appraisal and how a lender views future resale, so it's factored into underwriting on a case by case basis.

How much of my Toronto home's equity can I access?

It depends on your age, your home's appraised value, and current lender terms, so I won't estimate a number without those details. What I can tell you is the general range and walk you through real figures for your specific home during a free consultation. A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against your home. It must be repaid when you sell your home, move out permanently, or pass away. Interest accrues over time and reduces the equity remaining in your home. Terms, conditions, and lender qualification apply; approval, rates, and amounts are never guaranteed. A reverse mortgage is not a government program or benefit.
