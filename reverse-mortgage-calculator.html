Why you cannot borrow it all, and why that protects you

Canadian reverse mortgage lenders cap what you can borrow. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada says you may usually borrow up to 55% of the current value of the home. That cap is part of why, when the loan terms are met, you generally will not owe more than the home's fair market value, and it means a share of the home's value stays yours as equity on day one.

The money you unlock is tax free, because it is your own equity, not income. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada states it does not affect Old Age Security or the Guaranteed Income Supplement either.

What affects your number

  • The age of the youngest owner on title. If more than one person owns the home, lenders use the age of the youngest owner. Older generally means a higher percentage.
  • Your home's value, confirmed by appraisal. Your estimate gives a starting point, but the final number comes from a professional appraisal of your home. Several lenders publish a minimum appraised value of $250,000.
  • Location. Where the home is matters. Homes in larger centres often qualify for more than homes in smaller or remote areas. Some lenders also limit where they lend.
  • Property type and condition. A detached home, a condo, a rural property or an acreage can each be treated differently.
  • Any existing mortgage or secured debt. A reverse mortgage typically pays off and closes any current mortgage, HELOC or other secured loan first, so that balance comes off the top. For many families that also ends a monthly mortgage payment.
  • Which lender and which product. Eligibility and maximums vary. I can compare options from more than one lender, without quoting interest rates here.

Common questions

How much can I borrow with a reverse mortgage in Ontario?

According to the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, you may usually borrow up to 55% of your home's current value. The actual amount depends on age, the home's type, condition and appraised value, and the lender, and is subject to appraisal and lender approval.

Is this reverse mortgage calculator a quote?

No. It shows a ballpark range based on published maximums. It is not a quote, an offer to lend or an approval. Your actual amount comes only from a lender after an application and an appraisal.

Why do you ask for the youngest owner on title?

If more than one person owns the home, lenders use the age of the youngest owner to set the amount, not the age of the older owner. All owners on title usually need to be 55 or older.

Does a reverse mortgage affect OAS or GIS?

The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada states that money borrowed through a reverse mortgage does not affect Old Age Security or Guaranteed Income Supplement benefits. Income earned by investing the funds may be treated differently.

What does a reverse mortgage cost?

Interest is usually higher than on a regular mortgage or a home equity line of credit, and interest is added to the balance, so the amount owed grows over time and the equity left in the home decreases. Other costs can include appraisal, setup, legal and closing fees, and prepayment charges. This page does not quote interest rates. Ask for all costs in writing.

Sources: Financial Consumer Agency of Canada, Reverse mortgages. Updated 22 November 2026.

A reverse mortgage is a loan secured against your home. It must be repaid when you sell your home, move out permanently, or pass away. Interest accrues over time and reduces the equity remaining in your home. Terms, conditions, and lender qualification apply; approval, rates, and amounts are never guaranteed. A reverse mortgage is not a government program or benefit.
